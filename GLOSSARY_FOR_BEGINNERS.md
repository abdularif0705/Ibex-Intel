## 🏢 SEC Filings (What Public Companies Must Disclose)

### What is the SEC?
**SEC = Securities and Exchange Commission**
- Government agency that regulates stock markets
- Forces public companies to disclose financial information
- Goal: Prevent fraud, ensure transparency for investors

**Simple Analogy:** The SEC is like a referee that makes sure companies don't lie to investors.

### EDGAR System
**EDGAR = Electronic Data Gathering, Analysis, and Retrieval**
- Website: https://www.sec.gov/edgar
- Free database where ALL public company filings are stored
- Anyone can search and read these documents

**What We Do:** Our platform automatically searches EDGAR for technology transformation mentions.

## 📄 The Three Main SEC Filings

### 10-K: Annual Report Card

**What It Is:**
The **complete annual report** that every public company must file within 60-90 days after their fiscal year ends.

**Size:** 100-300 pages (very detailed)

**What's Inside:**
- Complete financial statements (balance sheet, income statement, cash flow)
- Business description
- Risk factors
- Management discussion & analysis (MD&A)
- Executive compensation
- Legal proceedings
- **Technology investments and major projects**

**Example from a Real 10-K:**
```
Item 7: Management's Discussion and Analysis

Technology Transformation Initiatives

During fiscal year 2024, we continued our multi-year 
investment in upgrading our enterprise resource planning 
(ERP) systems to SAP S/4HANA. We incurred approximately 
$127 million in implementation costs and expect to 
complete the transition in Q3 2025. This transformation 
is critical to improving operational efficiency and 
enabling our digital strategy.
```

**Why This Matters to Us:**
Companies are **legally required** to disclose major technology spending. We search for keywords like "SAP," "implementation," "ERP transformation," "system modernization."

**Frequency:** Once per year

**Example:** Apple files 10-K around October every year (fiscal year ends in September)

### 10-Q: Quarterly Report Card

**What It Is:**
A **shorter version** of the annual report, filed every quarter (3 months).

**Size:** 40-80 pages

**Frequency:** 3 times per year
- Q1 report (filed ~May)
- Q2 report (filed ~August)  
- Q3 report (filed ~November)
- No Q4 report (the 10-K covers that)

**What's Inside:**
- Quarterly financial statements (unaudited)
- Updates on business operations
- New risks or changes
- Updates on major projects

**Example from a Real 10-Q:**
```
Update on Technology Initiatives

Our SAP S/4HANA implementation remains on schedule 
for Q4 2024 go-live. We have completed user acceptance 
testing (UAT) and are currently in final data migration 
preparation. Implementation costs for the quarter were 
$31 million.
```

**Why This Matters to Us:**
10-Q filings give us **progress updates** on implementations. We can track:
- Is the project on schedule?
- Are costs increasing?
- Are there delays or problems?

**Red Flags We Look For:**
- "Implementation challenges"
- "Delayed timeline"
- "Additional costs"
- "System integration issues"

### 8-K: Breaking News Report

**What It Is:**
A **"current report"** filed when something major happens that investors need to know about immediately.

**Frequency:** Whenever something important happens (could be 0 times a year, could be 20 times)

**Size:** Usually 5-20 pages

**What Triggers an 8-K Filing:**
- CEO/CFO resignation or hiring
- Major acquisition announced
- Bankruptcy filing
- Change in accounting firm
- **Major contract signed**
- Significant business disruption
- Financial restatements

**Example 8-K:**
```
Item 1.01 Entry into a Material Definitive Agreement

On November 10, 2024, ABC Corporation entered into 
a Master Services Agreement with Deloitte Consulting LLP 
for enterprise resource planning (ERP) implementation 
services. The agreement has an initial term of 24 months 
with an estimated value of $85 million.
```

**Why This Matters to Us:**
8-K filings often reveal:
- When a company signs a consulting contract (proof of active implementation)
- When executives leave (CIO/CTO departures during implementation = red flag)
- Major problems ("we're delaying our system go-live")

**How We Use It:**
We search all 8-K filings for technology-related keywords.

## 🏆 Stock Market & Finance Terms

### Ticker Symbol
**What It Is:** The short code that identifies a company's stock.

**Examples:**
- AAPL = Apple
- MSFT = Microsoft  
- NKE = Nike
- TSLA = Tesla
- SAP = SAP (yes, the company SAP's ticker is literally SAP)

**Why We Use It:**
Easier to search "NKE" than "Nike Inc" (which could return Nike stores, Nike products, etc.)

### Public Company vs Private Company

**Public Company:**
- Sells stock on public exchanges (NYSE, NASDAQ)
- Anyone can buy shares
- **Must file SEC reports** (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K)
- Examples: Apple, Microsoft, Target

**Private Company:**
- Owned by founders, private investors, or private equity firms
- Does NOT trade on stock exchanges
- **Does NOT have to disclose finances publicly**
- Examples: SpaceX, Stripe, Cargill

**Why This Matters:**
We can easily track public companies (SEC filings). Private companies are harder (must rely on job postings, news, LinkedIn).

### Market Cap (Market Capitalization)

**What It Is:** The total value of all a company's stock.

**Formula:** Stock Price × Number of Shares

**Example:**
- Nike stock price: $100
- Nike has 1.5 billion shares
- Market Cap: $100 × 1.5B = **$150 billion**

**Categories:**
- **Mega Cap:** $200B+ (Apple, Microsoft, Amazon)
- **Large Cap:** $10B-200B (Nike, Target, Costco)
- **Mid Cap:** $2B-10B
- **Small Cap:** $300M-2B

**Why This Matters:**
Larger companies do bigger implementations. A failed SAP implementation at a $100B company can wipe out $10B-20B in market cap.

### Short Selling

**What It Is:** Betting that a stock price will GO DOWN.

**How It Works:**
1. Borrow 1,000 shares of Nike stock (currently $100/share)
2. Sell them immediately for $100,000
3. Wait for stock to drop to $80/share
4. Buy back 1,000 shares for $80,000
5. Return shares to lender
6. **Profit: $20,000**

**Why This Matters to Us:**
Hedge funds will pay us to identify companies with hidden risks (like risky implementations) so they can short the stock.

### Credit Rating / Credit Spread

**Credit Rating:** A grade (like AAA, AA, A, BBB, BB, B) that says how likely a company is to pay back its debts.

**Credit Spread:** The extra interest rate a company must pay because of risk.

**Example:**
- US Treasury Bond: 4% interest (considered "risk-free")
- Nike Corporate Bond: 5% interest
- **Credit Spread: 1%** (the extra 1% is compensation for Nike potentially defaulting)

**What Happens During Failed Implementation:**
- Company spends $200M on failed SAP project
- Company now has less cash
- Credit rating agencies downgrade from A to BBB
- Credit spread widens from 1% to 2%
- Existing bonds lose value

**Why This Matters to Us:**
Credit analysts will pay us to identify implementation risks that could lead to downgrades.

## 💼 Job Market & Recruiting Terms

### Contract-to-Hire

**What It Is:** A temporary job that might become permanent.

**How It Works:**
- Hired as contractor for 6-12 months
- If you do well, company converts you to full-time employee
- If project ends or you're not a fit, contract just ends

**Why Companies Use This:**
For risky implementations, they don't want to commit to permanent hires. If the project fails, they can easily let contractors go.

**Why This Matters to Us:**
Seeing "contract-to-hire" for SAP roles = company is uncertain about long-term success = risk indicator.

### System Integrator (SI)

**What It Is:** A consulting company that helps implement enterprise software.

**Major System Integrators:**
- **Deloitte** - One of the "Big 4" accounting/consulting firms
- **Accenture** - Largest pure consulting firm
- **IBM** - Both sells software and does consulting
- **Capgemini** - European consulting giant
- **PwC** (PricewaterhouseCoopers) - Another "Big 4"
- **KPMG** - Another "Big 4"
- **EY** (Ernst & Young) - Another "Big 4"

**What They Do:**
- Design how the new ERP system will work
- Customize the software to company's needs
- Migrate data from old system to new system
- Train employees
- Manage the project
- Handle the cutover weekend

**Typical Team:**
- 50-200 consultants on a large project
- Cost: $20M-100M+ just in consulting fees

**Why This Matters to Us:**
When we see job postings from Deloitte hiring SAP consultants for "confidential client," we know there's an active implementation somewhere.

### ATS (Applicant Tracking System)

**What It Is:** Software companies use to manage job applications.

**Major ATS Platforms:**
- **Greenhouse** - Modern, popular with tech companies
- **Ashby** - Newer, growing fast
- **Lever** - Common at startups
- **Workday Recruiting** - For companies using Workday HCM
- **Taleo** (Oracle) - Older, but still widely used

**Why This Matters:**
Each ATS platform structures job postings differently. Our scraping needs to handle all formats.

**Example:**
- Greenhouse URL: `company.greenhouse.io/jobs/123456`
- Ashby URL: `jobs.ashbyhq.com/company/job-id`

## 🔧 Technical & Implementation Terms

### Go-Live

**What It Is:** The day when the new system goes into production and employees start using it.

**Simple Analogy:** 
Like opening day for a new restaurant. All the preparation is done, now you're serving real customers with real consequences.

**Why It's Terrifying:**
- If the old system is turned off, there's no going back
- All employees must use new system immediately
- If it doesn't work, business stops
- Usually happens on a Monday morning

**Example Timeline:**
- Friday 6pm: Turn off old system
- Friday-Sunday: Cutover activities
- Monday 7am: **Go-Live** → employees arrive and must use new system

**Red Flag Keywords:**
- "Go-live delayed"
- "Pushed back go-live"  
- "Postponed launch"

### Cutover

**What It Is:** The transition period between turning off the old system and turning on the new system.

**Usually Happens:** Over a weekend (Friday evening to Sunday evening)

**Cutover Activities:**
1. Final data migration from old system to new system
2. Final testing and validation
3. Configuration of new system
4. Rollback testing (making sure we CAN go back if needed)
5. Final security checks
6. Communication to all employees

**Cutover Roles (job titles we look for):**
- **Cutover Manager** - Person in charge of the entire weekend
- **Cutover Lead** - Senior person managing specific area
- **Cutover Coordinator** - Helps execute the plan

**Why This Matters:**
Finding a "Cutover Manager" job posting means go-live is **60-90 days away** (they hire this person shortly before).

### UAT (User Acceptance Testing)

**What It Is:** Testing where actual employees (not IT people) try using the new system with real business scenarios.

**When It Happens:** 2-4 months before go-live

**Example UAT Scenario:**
```
Test Case 47: Process a Customer Return
Actor: Store Manager Sarah
Steps:
1. Open customer record in new SAP system
2. Look up original order
3. Process return
4. Issue refund
5. Update inventory

Expected Result: Refund processed, inventory updated, 
customer record reflects return

Actual Result: System error "Cannot find order" - FAILED
```

**Types of Testing:**
- **Unit Testing:** Developers test individual features
- **Integration Testing:** Test that different parts work together
- **System Testing:** Test the whole system
- **UAT:** Real users test real scenarios
- **Performance Testing:** Test if system can handle load

**Why This Matters:**
Seeing "UAT" in job postings or LinkedIn posts = late-stage implementation (70-80% complete).

### SIT (System Integration Testing)

**What It Is:** Testing that the new system connects properly to all the other systems it needs to talk to.

**Example:**
New SAP system must connect to:
- Warehouse management system
- E-commerce website
- Payment processors
- Shipping carriers
- Accounting system
- BI/Analytics platform

**SIT Tests Each Connection:**
"When we create an order in SAP, does it automatically send to the warehouse system?"

**When It Happens:** 3-5 months before go-live

### Rollback Plan

**What It Is:** The emergency procedure to go back to the old system if go-live fails.

**Example Rollback Scenario:**
```
Monday 7am: New SAP system goes live
Monday 9am: Orders aren't processing, inventory is wrong
Monday 11am: DECISION: Rollback
Monday 11am-2pm: Restore old system from backup
Monday 2pm: Back on old system
```

**Why It's Hard:**
- Must restore data that changed during go-live
- Some changes can't be undone
- Costs millions (failed weekend, restart later)
- Career-ending for project managers

**Why This Matters:**
Seeing "rollback plan" or "rollback testing" = extremely late stage, very high risk period.

### Dress Rehearsal / Mock Cutover

**What It Is:** A practice run of the cutover weekend.

**How It Works:**
- Usually done 2-4 weeks before actual go-live
- Team pretends it's the real cutover weekend
- Goes through every step
- Times how long each task takes
- Identifies problems

**Why Companies Do This:**
Find problems when stakes are low, not during real cutover.

**Why This Matters:**
Seeing "mock cutover" = go-live is 2-4 weeks away = maximum confidence signal.

## 🎯 Business Process Terms

### P2P (Procure-to-Pay)

**What It Is:** The complete process of buying things the company needs.

**Full Process:**
1. **Procure:** Employee needs office supplies
2. Create purchase requisition
3. Get manager approval
4. Send purchase order to vendor
5. Vendor ships products
6. Receive products in warehouse
7. Verify receipt matches order
8. Vendor sends invoice
9. **Pay:** Accounting pays invoice
10. Record in financial system

**Why ERP Is Critical Here:**
One system must track all 10 steps, ensure nothing falls through cracks.

**When P2P Fails:**
- Pay for things you never received
- Don't pay for things you did receive (angry vendors)
- Can't track spending
- Fraud becomes easy

**Why This Matters:**
"P2P" in job postings = company is implementing ERP finance/procurement modules.

### R2R (Record-to-Report)

**What It Is:** The complete process of financial accounting.

**Full Process:**
1. **Record:** Business transactions happen (sales, expenses, etc.)
2. Transactions recorded in ERP system
3. Journal entries created
4. General ledger updated
5. Reconciliations performed
6. Financial statements generated
7. **Report:** CFO reports results to investors

**Example:**
- Store makes $1M in sales today
- ERP records all transactions
- At month-end, accounting "closes the books"
- CFO can see: Total revenue, expenses, profit

**When R2R Fails:**
- Can't close the books (don't know if you made money)
- Can't file taxes
- Can't report earnings (stock market violation)
- Can't make business decisions (blind)

**Why This Matters:**
"R2R" in job postings = company is implementing ERP financial modules (very high risk).

### O2C (Order-to-Cash)

**What It Is:** The complete sales process.

**Full Process:**
1. **Order:** Customer places order
2. Check inventory availability
3. Check customer credit limit
4. Create sales order in system
5. Pick products from warehouse
6. Ship products
7. Generate invoice
8. **Cash:** Customer pays invoice
9. Apply payment to customer account
10. Record revenue

**Example:**
- You order $500 of products from Nike.com
- Nike's ERP system processes your order
- Warehouse ships shoes
- Nike sends invoice
- You pay
- Nike records $500 revenue

**When O2C Fails:**
- Can't process orders (lost sales)
- Ship wrong products (angry customers)
- Don't invoice (lost revenue)
- Can't track payments (lost money)

### Q2C (Quote-to-Cash)

**What It Is:** Like O2C but includes the sales/quoting process before the order.

**Full Process:**
1. **Quote:** Salesperson creates quote
2. Negotiate with customer
3. Customer accepts quote
4. Convert quote to sales order
5. Fulfill order (same as O2C)
6. **Cash:** Collect payment

**Used For:** B2B sales where every deal is custom priced.

**Example:**
Boeing selling a 787 to United Airlines:
- Quote: $150M per plane
- Negotiate: Final price $145M
- Order: United buys 10 planes
- Deliver planes over 3 years
- Collect payments

### MDM (Master Data Management)

**What It Is:** Keeping all core business data clean, consistent, and synchronized.

**Master Data Types:**
- **Customer Master:** All customer records
- **Vendor Master:** All supplier records
- **Product Master:** All product/SKU data
- **Employee Master:** All employee records
- **Location Master:** All store/warehouse/office locations

**The Problem:**
Old systems have messy data:
- Customer "John Smith" vs "J. Smith" vs "Smith, John"
- Product "Widget-A" vs "WDGT-A" vs "Widget A"
- Duplicate records everywhere
- Inconsistent formats

**What MDM Does:**
- Establishes one "golden record" for each entity
- Synchronizes data across all systems
- Enforces data quality rules
- Prevents duplicates

**Why This Matters:**
"MDM" in job postings = company is preparing for data migration = implementation is active.

### Data Migration / Data Cleansing

**Data Migration:** Moving data from old system to new system.

**The Challenge:**
- Old system: 30 years of messy data (millions of records)
- New system: Clean structure, different format
- Must map old to new
- Must clean errors
- Can't lose anything critical

**Data Cleansing Steps:**
1. **Extract:** Pull data from old system
2. **Clean:** Fix errors, remove duplicates
3. **Transform:** Convert to new format
4. **Validate:** Verify data is correct
5. **Load:** Insert into new system
6. **Reconcile:** Prove old = new

**Common Problems:**
- Old system had no validation (garbage in, garbage stays)
- Different systems defined things differently
- Can't decide which duplicate record is "correct"
- Special characters break new system
- Migration takes 10x longer than estimated

**Why This Matters:**
"Data Migration Lead" job postings = high-risk phase of implementation.

## 🏗️ Technical Architecture Terms

### API (Application Programming Interface)

**What It Is:** A way for two software systems to talk to each other.

**Simple Analogy:** 
An API is like a waiter at a restaurant:
- You (System A) tell the waiter what you want
- Waiter takes request to kitchen (System B)
- Kitchen prepares food
- Waiter brings food back to you

**Example:**
```
Your E-Commerce Site (System A):
"Hey SAP, I need inventory count for Product #1234"

API Request →

SAP System (System B):
"Product #1234 has 47 units in stock"

API Response →

Your Site: "Shows '47 In Stock' to customer"
```

**Why This Matters:**
Modern implementations require dozens of APIs. Our platform searches for "API integration" as a keyword.

### Middleware

**What It Is:** Software that sits between two systems and helps them communicate.

**Simple Analogy:**
Like a translator between people who speak different languages.

**Example:**
```
Old System: Speaks "mainframe language"
   ↓
Middleware: Translates between old and new
   ↓
New SAP System: Speaks "modern API language"
```

**Popular Middleware:**
- **Boomi** (Dell)
- **MuleSoft** (Salesforce)
- **Azure Logic Apps** (Microsoft)

**Why This Matters:**
We search for "Boomi" and "MuleSoft" as keywords indicating active integrations.

### Cloud vs On-Premises

**On-Premises (Old Way):**
- Company owns physical servers
- Servers sit in company's data center
- Company pays for everything (servers, cooling, electricity, IT staff)
- Company responsible for security, backups, updates

**Cloud (New Way):**
- Rent computing power from AWS/Azure/Google
- No physical servers to manage
- Pay monthly based on usage
- Provider handles security, backups, updates

**Why Companies Move to Cloud:**
- Lower upfront costs (no $10M data center)
- Scalability (rent more power during busy times)
- Automatic updates
- Better disaster recovery

**Why It's Risky:**
- Must migrate everything without downtime
- Different security model (trusting cloud provider)
- Potentially higher long-term costs
- Lock-in to cloud provider

**Examples:**
- **SAP S/4HANA Cloud** - Cloud version
- **SAP ECC** - On-premises version
- **Workday** - Cloud-only (no on-premises option)

### Legacy System / Legacy Decommissioning

**Legacy System:** Old software the company currently uses (often 20-30 years old).

**Why Companies Keep Legacy Systems:**
- They work (mostly)
- Employees know how to use them
- Expensive/risky to replace
- Contains decades of custom code

**Decommissioning:** Turning off the old system after migration.

**Why It's Hard:**
- Some people still trying to access old system
- Might need old data for audits/legal (must archive)
- Custom integrations must be rebuilt
- "Hidden" dependencies discovered too late

**Why This Matters:**
"Legacy decommissioning" = late-stage implementation activity.

## 📊 Data & Analytics Terms

### BI (Business Intelligence)

**What It Is:** Software that turns raw business data into reports, dashboards, and insights.

**Popular BI Tools:**
- **Tableau** - Visual dashboards
- **Power BI** (Microsoft) - Excel-like interface
- **Looker** (Google) - Modern, SQL-based
- **SAP BusinessObjects** - Enterprise BI

**Example Use:**
CEO wants to see: "Sales by region, by product, by month" → BI tool creates dashboard.

**Why This Matters:**
BI tools must connect to ERP. If ERP implementation fails, BI breaks too.

### Data Warehouse / Data Lake

**Data Warehouse:** 
Centralized database that stores data from all company systems for analysis.

**Data Lake:**
Like a warehouse but stores raw, unstructured data.

**Example:**
```
Sources:
- ERP system data
- CRM system data  
- Website clickstream data
- Social media data
↓
All flows into Data Warehouse
↓
BI tools analyze the warehouse
↓
Executive dashboards
```

**Why This Matters:**
ERP implementations often include "modernizing the data warehouse."

## 💰 Financial Terms

### CAPEX vs OPEX

**CAPEX (Capital Expenditure):**
- Money spent on assets that last multiple years
- Example: Buying servers for $10M
- Accounting: Depreciate over 5-10 years

**OPEX (Operating Expenditure):**
- Money spent on day-to-day operations
- Example: Paying $100K/month for cloud services
- Accounting: Expense immediately each month

**Why Cloud Shifted This:**
- Old way: Buy servers (CAPEX)
- New way: Rent cloud (OPEX)
- CFOs like OPEX (doesn't tie up cash upfront)

**Why This Matters:**
When companies move to cloud ERP, they switch from CAPEX to OPEX model.

### ROI (Return on Investment)

**What It Is:** How much value you get back for money spent.

**Formula:** (Gain - Cost) / Cost × 100%

**Example:**
- Company spends $100M on SAP implementation
- SAP promises $30M/year in cost savings
- ROI = ($30M - $100M) / $100M = -70% (year 1)
- After 4 years: ($120M - $100M) / $100M = 20%

**The Problem:**
ERP vendors promise amazing ROI, but most companies never achieve it (projects fail or go over budget).

### Write-Off / Write-Down

**What It Is:** Admitting you spent money on something worthless.

**Example:**
- Company spends $150M on SAP project
- Project fails after 2 years
- Must "write off" the $150M
- This hits the income statement as a loss
- Stock price drops

**Famous Example:**
Lidl (German retailer) wrote off **€500M** in failed SAP implementation costs in 2018.

## 🎓 Professional Roles & Titles

### C-Suite Executives

**CEO (Chief Executive Officer):** 
The boss. Makes final decisions.

**CFO (Chief Financial Officer):**
In charge of all money. Worried about ERP costs and financial risks.

**CTO (Chief Technology Officer):**
In charge of all technology strategy. Often drives ERP decisions.

**CIO (Chief Information Officer):**
In charge of IT operations. Actually manages ERP implementation.

**COO (Chief Operating Officer):**
In charge of day-to-day operations. Cares if new ERP helps efficiency.

**Why This Matters:**
- CTO/CIO departure during implementation = major red flag
- New CIO often wants to "review" previous CIO's projects = delay risk

- Designs how new system will work
- Makes high-level technical decisions
- $150-250/hour as consultant

**Functional Consultant:**

- $100-200/hour

**Technical Consultant:**
- Writes custom code
- Builds integrations
- $100-200/hour

**Project Manager:**
- Keeps project on track
- Manages timeline and budget
- Communicates with executives
- $120-200/hour

**Change Management Consultant:**
- Helps employees adapt to change
- Trains users
- Manages resistance
- $80-150/hour

**Data Migration Specialist:**
- Moves data from old to new system
- $100-180/hour

**Basis Administrator (SAP-specific):**
- Manages SAP technical infrastructure
